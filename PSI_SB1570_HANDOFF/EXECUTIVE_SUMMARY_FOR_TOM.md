# What SB 1570 Means for Tom and Performance Services

SB 1570 created something commercially meaningful but narrower than a pipeline: Illinois municipalities and school districts may choose a single design-build contract for design and construction. If they choose that route, foundation Public Act 103-0491 requires structured notice, scope development, qualifications, technical/cost evaluation, conflict protections, and reporting. Public Act 104-0395 amended the municipal selection procedure effective July 1, 2025: the ordinary Phase II shortlist remains two to six, but a municipality receiving only one Phase I response may evaluate that respondent in Phase II when it finds, in its discretion, that proceeding is in its best interest. Neither Act compels adoption, appropriates money, identifies projects, or guarantees savings.

Current law checked through P.A. 104-0395. Official sources: [foundation P.A. 103-0491](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/PublicActs/PrinterFriendly/103-0491) and [current amendment P.A. 104-0395](https://www.ilga.gov/Legislation/PublicActs/PrinterFriendly/104-0395).

For Performance Services, the strongest opening is upstream of the RFP. By public notice, the owner has already selected a delivery method and developed enough schedule, budget, funding, scope, and performance criteria to solicit proposals. PSI should therefore reconstruct the earlier public story: facility need, deferred maintenance, energy or capacity problem, capital-plan appearance, feasibility or facility study, funding formation, professional-services selection, and governing-body discussion of delivery options. None of those traces is guaranteed by this Act; each must be sourced and labeled as an investigative hypothesis.

Tom should spend relationship time when several independent signals converge: a consequential documented need, an eligible owner, a plausible capital horizon, evidence that funding or financing is forming, decision-maker attention, and a delivery path still open. A single agenda mention or aging building is not enough. PSI should show the evidence chain and what remains missing.

Performance Services should prepare for the statute's qualifications-first competition: personnel and similar-project experience, financial capacity, timeliness, references, committed team and consultants, utilization-goal performance, technical quality, innovation, constructability, cost, and completion time. Price cannot be used in municipal or school Phase I; total project cost may carry no more than 30% in Phase II.

The most important boundary is ethical as well as commercial. The professional who prepares statutory scope and performance criteria cannot participate in a proposal for that project. Relationships or circumstances creating an advantage or appearance of impropriety may also disqualify an entity. PSI should flag potentially conflicted early involvement; it must not turn “educate the owner” into covert procurement shaping. Questions about a particular interaction belong with Illinois procurement counsel.

Bottom line: the Act enlarges the set of legally possible pursuits. PSI's value is proving which owners are moving from need to funded, delivery-ready opportunity early enough for lawful human action—and refusing to call permission demand.
